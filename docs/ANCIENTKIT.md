# ANCIENTKIT: ancient world / ruins kits in the Ocarina-era N64 look

ANCIENTKIT adds two style profiles and four kit families for sun-baked temple cities, carved stone, and desert and
jungle ruins:

- **Style profiles:** `ancient_ruins` and `jungle_temple`.
- **Kit families:** `ruin.` architecture, `relic.` carving props, `wild.` nature, `land.` terrain and sky sets.

Everything is original:
- Invented figures: the robed **Keeper** holding a sun disc under a fan crown, and the seated **Crested Guardian**.
- An original glyph grammar on a 3×3 lattice. It is no real script.
- No real-culture caricature and no franchise shapes, names or logos.

Generators are deterministic Python 3.9 + numpy. Textures are painted by code, vertex light and AO are baked, and
every GLB is validated. Pieces recolour through palette roles. They interlock on the **RUIN GRID**, and a socket
manifest describes how they connect.

Result, numbers and known failures: `docs/ANCIENTKIT-RESULT.md`. Hub/catalog notes: `docs/ANCIENTKIT-HUB-ENTRIES.md`.
Critiques: `docs/ancientkit/CRITIQUE-1..7.md`. Mood-image check: `docs/ancientkit/REFPACK-CHECK.md`. Best renders:
`docs/ancientkit/best6.png`.

**Acceptance boundary:** the final style gate passes 32/32 room views, but one N64 close-up measure fails.
The court and jungle finish remain Main-review-required, not self-approved. Exact weak frames and hash-identical
review copies are linked in `docs/ancientkit/CRITIQUE-7.md`. Actual validator scope is mapped in
`docs/ancientkit/VALIDATOR-COVERAGE.md`.

## 2. Style profiles

Both profiles share the generators and differ in palette, roles, light and fog.

| | `ancient_ruins` | `jungle_temple` |
|---|---|---|
| Base | sun-baked sandstone (`sand_0..5`), weathered limestone/basalt carving (`carve_0..3`), gold leaf (`gold_0..2`) | mossy temple stone (`mstone_0..5`), moss (`moss_0..3`), dark earth, gold idols |
| Sky | deep blue zenith (`sky_0`) to a warm dust haze (`haze #e4dccb`) | humid blue-green sky to a pale green haze (`#c9dccb`) |
| Shade | deep coloured shade: indigo `shade_0..2`, never black | green-black `shade_0..2` |
| Bake rig | key from the east-south-east `[0.6, -0.2, 0.77]`; ambient 0.38, key 0.6, AO 0.4 | key from the west `[-0.55, -0.2, 0.81]`; ambient 0.4, key 0.5, AO 0.4 |
| Runtime rig (exteriors) | key `sand_5` from the east-south-east, 0.56 on the sun road and 0.52 at the oasis and court; ambient `shade_2` 0.36 on the sun road (deep noon shade), 0.46 at the oasis and court; fill 0.12 (`sky_2` on the sun road, `water_2` at the oasis and court) | key `mstone_5` 0.46 from the west; ambient `mstone_3` 0.56; fill `sky_2` 0.24 from the south-east, opposite the key, so faces turned from the sun keep their carving |
| Exterior fog | 8–38 m, max 1.0, colour = the rendered horizon | 7–34 m, max 1.0, colour = the rendered horizon |
| Interior (roofless sanctum) | fog `shade_1` 12–36 m, max 0.35; the open vault shows the sky | the same, green |

**Palette roles.** They cover all 32 colours of each palette, and every material ramp stays inside its roles: the
role audit runs in the library gates and in the tests.
- `ancient_ruins` roles: `shadow_indigo`, `sandstone`, `carved_stone`, `gold_leaf`, `deep_sky`, `vegetation`,
  `oasis_water`, `fire`.
- `jungle_temple` roles: `shadow_green`, `mossy_stone`, `moss`, `vines_leaves`, `earth`, `gold_leaf`, `humid_sky`,
  `jungle_water`, `fire`.

**Look profiles** (`view.json` → `look.profiles`):

| profile | use |
|---|---|
| `approach` | ancient_ruins: desert noon |
| `oasis` / `clearing` | the later, warmer sun, or jungle humidity |
| `shrine` / `interior` | the roofless sanctum |
| `exterior` | alias of the first exterior profile |

The jungle approach has its own softer grade: dark mossy silhouettes against pale fog were the frame's strongest
one-pixel edges.

**Fog equals the rendered horizon.** A painted sky renders at `lighting.unlit_level` (0.88) times the light
ramp's top step. Exterior fog and background therefore use `tools/ancientkit_style.rendered(haze)`, so fogged
distance, empty background and the painted horizon band are one colour (n64_fidelity `fog_horizon_delta`). The
plugin pack's `lighting.fog_colour` stays the palette haze, as its validator requires.

**Emitters.**
- Braziers, the fire altar and the awakened tablet are runtime point lights.
- The fire light is warm *white*. The orange stays in the flame, a small painted floor pool and a ≤ 1.2 m halo.

**Style-gate thresholds** (`view.json` → `style_gate`): core defaults except one documented definition. Sandstone
and mossy stone are themselves warm (hue 25–45°, sat 0.30–0.62), so the accent family is saturated flame/ember (sat
≥ 0.70, hue 10–50°), and its budget is *tightened* from 15% to 6%. The existing warm-earth pack `n64_v0` does the
same (sat ≥ 0.55, hue 38–58°). No other threshold differs from core; `tests/test_ancientkit.py` asserts this.

## 3. The RUIN GRID

| | |
|---|---|
| Cell | 4 m × 4 m. Grid pieces turn in 90° steps. |
| Walls | Closed solids 1.0 m thick, centred on the cell boundary (inner face 0.5 m in). Storey 4.5 m. |
| Doorway | Cut 2.8 × 3.6 m, clear frame 2.0 × 3.0 m. room.gd's door anchor sits 0.4 m in from the room edge at `door.position = 4i + 2`. |
| Room plot | cells × 4 m + 0.5 m apron each side. |
| Stairs | rise 0.25 m, run 0.4 m. Pyramid tier 2.0 m. |
| Water | surface at −0.25 m (oasis). |

The numbers match the DUNGEON GRID, so the two kits can share plots.

**Anchors.**
- `cell_block_centre`: ground, floors, courts, pyramids, terraces, the oasis.
- `cell_centre`: stairs, slopes, causeways.
- `cell_edge`: walls, ramparts, cliffs.
- `corner`: piers.
- `door_anchor`: gates.
- `wall_face`: banners, vines.
- `free`: statues, columns, nature.

**Connectors.**

| connector | rule |
|---|---|
| `edge` ↔ `edge` | walls and ramparts meet end to end on the half-cell lattice; perpendicular ends meet at a corner pier |
| `ground_edge` ↔ `ground_edge` | tiles, oasis, wash and terrace bases meet on the datum |
| `door` ↔ `ruin.gate` | a door socket takes exactly one gate frame |
| `corner` ↔ `ruin.pier` | a pier covers the joint where two walls meet |
| stair / slope / terrace sockets | climb levels |
| `wall` ↔ wall mounts | banners and vines hang on wall sockets |

`tools/ancientkit_rooms.py` enforces these before anything is written:
- every declared door lies on a door socket facing the plot, and every cut is used;
- edge pieces chain on the half-cell lattice and never overlap;
- grid pieces snap (cell, block, edge, corner) and never sit at free metres or odd angles;
- wall mounts hang on wall sockets;
- there is exactly one landmark per region;
- the triangle estimate (pieces + door frames + sky + 760 per person) is ≤ 6,000.

## 6. Using the kits in a game

1. Pick the style pack, `ancient_ruins` or `jungle_temple`. Its `bindings.json` maps every kit key to a
   hash-pinned GLB. `arch.door` is bound to the `ruin.gate` frame, and `sky.backdrop` to `land.sky__mesas` or
   `land.sky__canopy`.
2. Write a room plan: kit keys and grid placements. For example `{"key": "ruin.wall__door", "edge": [2, 1, "e"]}`,
   `{"key": "ruin.floor", "cell": [1, 1]}` or `{"key": "relic.statue__guardian", "at_m": [6.2, 9.0], "yaw": 90}`.
3. Compile it with `tools.ancientkit_rooms.compile_room(plan)`. You get a spec room, its presentation row
   (`kit_shell: true`) and a report. A broken connector raises `PlanError` with the reason.
4. The demo (`tools/ancientkit_demo.py`) is the worked example: four regions linked by doors, one with a flag
   condition (the pressure plate unseals the sanctum's east door).
